Most of what I do

Septic repairs

An alarm going off doesn't mean your system has failed. Most of the time one part has stopped working, and the part costs a fraction of what people are braced for.

I find out what's wrong before anyone talks about replacing it

The most expensive thing in septic work is a wrong guess. If someone assumes the pump is dead and swaps it, you've paid for a pump and you still have the original problem.

So I check first. That takes longer and it costs you less.

What that looks like

“Instead of just assuming the pump was bad and replacing it, he took the time to troubleshoot. Jim determined that the pump was clogged and saw that the power cord was sloppily spliced with electrical tape… A new pump was not necessary, and just like that he saved me $1600.” — Kez B.

What I repair

  • Pumps and floats. Clogs, bad splices, failed floats. Checked before replaced.
  • Control panels. Rewiring, new panel and post where the old one has corroded.
  • Distribution boxes. Concrete d-boxes crumble and send all the flow down one line.
  • Drain lines. Cracked or collapsed sections replaced.
  • Pressure manifolds.
  • Filtration systems. Replacing old setups with the current standard.
  • Risers and lids. Including repairing cracked ones by plastic welding rather than replacing.
