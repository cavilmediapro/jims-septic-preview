Most of what I do

Septic repairs

An alarm going off doesn’t mean your system has failed. Most of the time one part has stopped working, and the part costs a fraction of what people are braced for.

An alarm is information, not a verdict. It tells you the water is higher than it should be. It does not tell you why, and the why is the whole job.

How a call goes

I find out what’s wrong before anyone talks about replacing it.

I come out, open the lid and test the system rather than guessing at it. The pump comes out and gets checked. The floats get checked. The panel gets checked. The filter gets pulled. If the problem is further down, the distribution box and the lines get opened up too.

You watch all of it. By the time I give you a number, you’ve seen the thing the number is for.

What I repair

  • Pumps, float switches and pressure manifolds
  • Control panels, alarms and wiring
  • Effluent filters — cleaned, and often that is the entire problem
  • Distribution boxes, including replacing one that has crumbled
  • Drain lines and field lines, repaired or re-established
  • Risers and lids, including plastic welding a cracked one rather than replacing it

The quote is the quote

You get the price before the work starts, and that is the price. One customer put it plainly: “He stuck to his word on pricing and only charged me what he originally said he would. Even though he had to do additional work.”

If someone else has already been out
