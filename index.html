Why a second opinion is worth the call

The expensive answer and the honest answer look the same from your driveway.

A drain field replacement in Johnston County runs into five figures. A pump is a few hundred dollars. A clogged effluent filter is an afternoon. From where you’re standing — alarm going, water where it shouldn’t be — those three look identical, and you have no way to tell which one you’re being quoted for.

That asymmetry is the whole problem with septic work. It isn’t that most companies are dishonest. It’s that nobody has ever shown you your own system, so you can’t check the answer you’re given.

So the first thing I do is dig the lid out and walk you through what’s down there. You see the pump come out. You see whether the filter is packed. You see whether the roots are in the lines or whether the field is genuinely finished. Then we talk about money, and you can tell whether the number makes sense.

Sometimes the answer really is the expensive one, and I’ll tell you that too. But it should be the conclusion, not the opening bid.
