Before anyone quotes you a new field

Blockages and hydro jetting

A drain field that has stopped taking water is not always a drain field that has failed. Very often it’s full of roots.

A blocked line and a dead field look exactly the same from the surface. One is an afternoon. The other is five figures. It is worth knowing which you have.

What is actually happening

What makes a line block up.

Three things, mostly. Roots find the pipe and grow into it, because a drain line is the wettest, richest thing in your yard. Sludge carries over from a tank that has gone too long without attention. And an effluent filter nobody has ever cleaned slowly chokes the flow before it reaches the field at all.

None of those three is a failed drain field. All three get mistaken for one.

What I do about it

  • Open the distribution box and find out which line is actually blocked
  • Hydro jet the line — high-pressure water that cuts the roots and sludge out
  • Re-establish the line and confirm it is taking water again
  • Clean or replace the effluent filter while I’m there, because it is usually part of the story

When it really is the field

Sometimes it is, and I’ll say so. But that should be what we conclude after looking, not what we assume before. A customer put it this way: “He went above and beyond and also provided some great maintenance advice for the future. It’s clear Jim cares about his work and is as honest as they get.”
