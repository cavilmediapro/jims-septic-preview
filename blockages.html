Before anyone quotes you a new field

Blockages and hydro jetting

A drain field that has stopped taking water is not always a drain field that has failed. Very often it's full of roots.

This came out of one line

Tree roots find the water in your field lines and grow into them. They keep growing. Eventually the line stops carrying anything, water backs up toward the house or surfaces in the yard, and it looks exactly like a system that needs replacing.

Hydro jetting cuts the roots out under pressure and re-establishes the line. It is a fraction of the cost of a new drain field.

“I had him come over to hydro jet field lines that were blocked by roots and some sludge. He went above and beyond and also provided some great maintenance advice/guidance for the future as well.” — Chris S., a week ago

What makes a line block up

  • Roots. The most common one, and the most dramatic to pull out.
  • Sludge. Solids that got past the tank, usually because the filter was never cleaned.
  • A failed distribution box. When the d-box crumbles, one line takes all the flow and drowns while the others stay dry.
  • A collapsed section. Occasionally the pipe itself has given way and needs replacing.

What I do about it

Locate the field and the distribution box, find which line is the problem, jet it, and get the flow spread properly again. If the d-box is the cause, that gets replaced too — still a much smaller job than a new field.

When it really is the field

Sometimes it is. Fields do reach the end of their life, and I'll tell you if that's what I'm looking at. But I'd rather find that out after jetting a line than instead of it.
