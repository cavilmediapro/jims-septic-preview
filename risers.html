Risers

A riser brings the opening of your tank up to ground level, with a lid you can lift. Without one, every single visit — inspection, pump-out, repair — starts with digging.

Once risers are on, the tank is a thirty second job to open instead of an hour with a shovel.

Cracked lids and risers

They crack. The usual answer is to replace the whole thing. Often they can be plastic welded instead, which costs less and holds fine.

“We had some cracks in our risers and one riser lid on our septic tank… He successfully repaired the risers and one of the lids with plastic welding. He is extremely talented!” — Wyler Family

The effluent filter

There is a filter cartridge sitting in the outlet of most tanks. Its job is to stop solids getting out to the drain field, which is the part you really don't want to damage.

It needs pulling out and rinsing about once a year. When it clogs, the symptoms look alarming — slow drains, backing up, water where it shouldn't be — and people assume the worst.

What a clogged filter can look like

“We had some septic coming up out of my yard… Gary from the county came out and told me that it was just a clogged filter. I feel like I owe Jim something. He was such an amazing guy.” — Sara B.

Worth asking about

If you've just bought the house, risers and a filter clean are usually the two cheapest things that save you the most later.
